## Decisions, and questions still open

A settled design question gets an entry here in the same round it settles, without being asked: the decision and the
reasoning behind it, so a later reader finds the why and not only the what. A question that is open stays here too,
marked open, until it closes. An entry is never deleted; a reversed decision gets a new entry that says what changed
and why.

An entry holds the decision itself, in a sentence that stands alone. "Approved as recommended" is a label: it tells a
later reader that something was decided and not what, because the recommendation stayed in a conversation. Write what
was recommended, and what the person said, in their own words. A comment or a test that cites a decision names this
entry, by its date and its first words.

- **<The decision in a few words> (<date>).** <What was decided, in one or two sentences that need no other page.>
  <The person's own words, in quotation marks, when they decided it.> <Why: the constraint or the harm it avoids, in
  the words the question was asked in.> <What it covers, and what it leaves open.> <Where it shows: the file, the test
  or the screen.> <What was considered and not chosen, in one sentence, when that helps a later reader.>
- **Open: <the question in a few words> (<date raised>).** <What is unclear and what would settle it.> <The choices,
  each with its reason, and what was read for them, so that the question can be worked out again and not only
  repeated.>
